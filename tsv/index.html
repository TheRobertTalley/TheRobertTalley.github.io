OPERATOR REFERENCE

A few deliberate gestures.
A lot of control.

The non-dominant hand is your control hand. The layout shown above uses right-hand dominance; left-hand mode mirrors the controls and docked HUD.

Full controls reference ↗
01Move views & open Settings
  1. Use your non-dominant hand in the region you want to control.
  2. Hold an index pinch and rotate your wrist from palm-up to palm-down, or back again. A completed rotation steps the assigned view. Pinching alone does not advance it.
  3. Hold a middle-finger pinch and rotate to cycle supported secondary sources. Use one pinch command at a time.
  4. Triple-tap the underside of the opposite wrist to open or close Settings. Point with the tracked hand pointer and index-pinch to select a button.

In the default layout: map is upper left, FPV upper right, enhanced vision lower left, and the dedicated weapon-camera viewport lower right.

02Use thermal & night vision
  1. Connect a supported USB source, open the camera/source settings and select its physical device and role. Check the live image before enabling its overlay.
  2. For night vision, choose ON for manual use, AUTO for light-triggered activation, or OFF to hide the forward NV overlay. A separately selected mirror can still need the same camera.
  3. For thermal, select an image mode: Balanced, Target, Detail or Manual. Adjust palette and image tuning under THERMAL IMAGE.
  4. Use the lower-left control area for supported enhanced-vision commands. Align the forward overlay and select SAVE.

AUTO requires a working low-light source and valid light measurements. Thermal shows infrared contrast; it does not make the scene transparent. Check the view while moving your head after every mount or alignment change.

03Use FPV, camera & mirror views
  1. Connect the supported receiver and configure it to match your transmitter, or configure the dedicated camera video endpoint.
  2. Confirm actual moving video. A connected receiver alone does not establish a live picture.
  3. For primary FPV, use its control region to step through full → center → narrow mirror → HUD → hidden.
  4. Select Rear View Source in Settings to choose an available camera or the Radar source for the sensor mirror.

The dedicated weapon-camera viewport has its own hidden, HUD, center and full presentations. It displays video; mechanical pan/tilt and camera-view head tracking are not implemented in the documented build.

04Navigate & share map context
  1. Connect the configured GPS or Meshtastic position source and wait for a real position fix. Review its accuracy and age.
  2. Bring the map to the center. Begin a palm-down index pinch on the circular map, drag the marker preview and release to place it.
  3. Use supported location, route, target, landing-zone and medical markers to organize the map. Repeated route labels connect route points.
  4. On a configured mesh, use the held two-finger, knife-hand and closed-stop poses for the corresponding threat, direction and stop events. Those poses are disabled while Settings or the center map is active.

Headset placement that requires your location waits for valid GPS. Team sharing requires the configured private channel; a local marker or local deletion is not proof that every teammate received the change.

05Read radar, depth & visual cues
  1. Connect the supported sensor bridge and radar. Open Radar / Sensors to check the selected sensor, mounting and response settings.
  2. Choose Radar as the Rear View source to display its scope. LD2450 supplies up to three X/Y contacts; LD2410C supplies presence/range without bearing or a target count.
  3. For LD2450 tracking, read solid dots as fresh contacts and dim hollow dots as briefly held positions. Confirm left/right and forward direction against real movement.
  4. Enable the required Quest scene/camera access for supported depth and visual-awareness functions.

Radar does not classify people or pets and cannot acquire a new contact behind its forward-facing sensor. Quest depth is a scene estimate, not a laser measurement. Visual outlines and approach cues are advisory and can miss or misidentify objects.

06Calibrate & keep a known good setup
  1. Secure the camera mount. In CAMERA SETTINGS, select the physical source and correct its orientation.
  2. In SOURCE ALIGNMENT, match the overlay to real objects using position, rotation, scale and field of view. Use keystone, distortion and timing tools as the setup requires.
  3. Adjust thermal appearance in THERMAL IMAGE. Image tuning and overlay placement are separate controls.
  4. Check nearby and distant detail, both eyes and slow head movement. Select SAVE when the complete result works.
SAVE
Commit the complete settings group.
CANCEL / LAST USED
Restore the values active when Settings opened.
LAST KNOWN GOOD
Preview the previous committed group.
FACTORY DEFAULTS
Preview defaults. They become permanent only when saved.

When upgrading, use a compatible in-place installation to preserve app data. Do not uninstall to resolve a signing mismatch.

07Connect the browser operations page
  1. Open TSV Operations and add the headset’s LAN address or your configured private HTTPS relay.
  2. Select Connect All. Confirm the headset appears in Assets, then check radio status and GPS separately.
  3. Choose a marker type or Route and use the map tools. Sending requires a connected headset with the relevant capability.
  4. For text, open Messages, select Through headset and a Radio channel, compose the message and select Send Message when authorized messaging is ready.

The public page is an interface, not a public radio gateway. Browser local-network restrictions may require a compatible local access path or a configured HTTPS relay. Keep credentials out of share links; delivery status must confirm the outcome.

08When a source is missing or stale
  • No camera image: check hub power, cable, USB permission and the assigned physical device. Confirm the camera’s supported mode.
  • Overlay moves off the scene: check mount movement, working distance, alignment and timing. Recheck it while wearing the headset.
  • No map position: check the actual GPS or radio source. A working web connection does not create a position fix.
  • Messages unavailable: confirm the radio’s channel table and configured operator authentication.
  • Unexpected settings: preview LAST KNOWN GOOD, verify the result, then SAVE.

Stale or unavailable sources need attention even when another part of the workspace is working.
